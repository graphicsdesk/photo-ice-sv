In Focus: Thousands rally against ICE in wake of Marble Hill shooting

After speeches by local political organizers, protesters marched from Foley Square to 201 Varick St., which houses Immigration and Customs Enforcement offices.

By Eliza Lamster

Web Design by Audrea Chen

October 25th, 2026

An onlooker photographs Devashish Basnet, senior immigration policy strategist at the New York Civil Liberties Union, during his speech at the Oct. 9 protest. Georgia Paulos / Columbia Daily Spectator

Demonstrators gathered at Foley Square for an evening of protest on Oct. 9, following the shooting of a Marble Hill resident by Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers. As onlookers waited for speeches to begin, they mingled and chattered amongst themselves. One demonstrator held a banner calling for the immediate dissolution of ICE.

Photo by Georgia Paulos

Photo by Tianqi Wang

The rally was organized by Hands Off NYC, a grassroots coalition of local organizers, labor unions, and nonprofits “fighting for a future where all New Yorkers lead lives with safety and dignity.” It was set to begin at 5:30 pm with a series of speeches from local organizers and advocates, followed by a march from Foley Square to 201 Varick St., which holds Customs and Immigration Services offices and immigration court.

As they wait for speeches to begin, three demonstrators stand slightly apart from the crowd, holding signs reading “Spartacist” and condemning both ICE and the Democratic Party.

Photo by Conor Cavens

The rally came one day after Oscar Belgal, a 28-year old Dominican immigrant, was shot in his car by plainclothes ICE officers. According to comments from Marcos Charles, executive associate director of ICE’s Enforcement and Removal Operations,, “Belgal refused lawful orders to exit his vehicle” and “weaponized his vehicle and proceeded to accelerate his vehicle toward other officers.”

Photo by Megan Morey

The shooting occurred while Belgal’s son was in the back seat, a detail which speakers, demonstrators, and politicians have honed in on. “May the agonizing screams of that five-year-old keep the officers who shot those rounds up every single night,” Murad Awawdeh, president and CEO of the New York Immigration Coalition, said in his speech at the rally.

Photo by Eliza Lamster

Demonstrators listen intently to speeches in Foley Square. The plaza, which lies in the civic center of lower Manhattan, has been a popular locale for anti-ICE protests during President Donald Trump’s second term. It borders 26 Federal Plaza, which houses immigration court, a site from which ICE agents have seized multiple immigrants attending mandated hearings.

Photo by Tianqi Wang

Devashish Basnet, senior immigration policy strategist at the New York Civil Liberties Union, took the stage. “This shooting is what happens when a rogue, a racist, an unaccountable ICE operates freely on our streets,” he said. As Basnet called for the abolition of ICE, he was met with cheers from the crowd. Other speakers hailed from advocacy organizations like Make the Road New York and the New York Immigration Coalitions.

As the speeches came to an end, onlookers cheered and applauded from behind police barricades. In addition to calls for the abolition of ICE, many demanded that New York City Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch resign.

Photo by Conor Cavens

This sentiment was echoed by Darializa Avila Chevalier, CC ’16, the Democratic nominee for New York’s 13th congressional district. “The NYPD and Commissioner Tisch violated the spirit, if not the letter, of our sanctuary city policies and fueled the Trump Administration’s attempt to demonize our communities,” she wrote in an Oct. 9 X post.

Demonstrators leave Foley Square and enter Tribeca as they head west on Worth Street. In the middle of the crowd, a woman in a red shirt holds up a “Fuck ICE” sign.

Photo by Megan Morey

Photo by Georgia Paulos

Having amassed thousands of protesters, the rally turns onto Church Street and continues past the Anish Kapoor sculpture known as “The Bean,” a reference to a similar sculpture by the same artist in Chicago. Police officers flank the crowd as they begin to make their way north.

As the march continues up Church Street, protesters flood Tribeca, some holding large yellow and teal vertical banners exclaiming “New York is for all of us.”

Photo by Georgia Paulos

A woman near the front of the crowd blows into a long green vuvuzela; nearby, drummers and trumpeters also lend instrumentation to the protesters’ chants.

Photo by Eliza Lamster

Photo by Megan Morey

As the sun sets, three individuals walk together behind the rest of the group. Backlit by the headlights of a police car, they brandish a sign reading “ICE off our streets.” One woman has large pink earrings which read “Fuck ICE.”

A woman near the front of the march raises her fist in anger, showing off her sign which reads “ICE shot a dad in front of his kid!”

Photo by Conor Cavens

A woman stands away from the throng. Looking exhausted and angry, she holds up a black and white sign, asking, “Are your hearts severed?”

Photo by Conor Cavens

Photo by Megan Morey

As the protest reached 201 Varick St., its final destination and the site of the New York Varick Immigration Court, the frontmost banners emphasized the core messages of the demonstration: Immigrants, not ICE, are welcome in New York.

One protester, standing at the center of a circle of demonstrators, chanted into a bullhorn adorned with “Free Palestine” and anti-ICE stickers.
